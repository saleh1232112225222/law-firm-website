Saudi Business Regulations & Laws Library

A simplified interactive guide to core Saudi commercial and labor laws to protect your enterprise in Riyadh.

Corporate Law

New Saudi Companies Law (1444H / 2022G)

A strategic legislative leap designed to simplify corporate procedures, foster investment flow, and support startup/family businesses by removing regulatory hurdles and enhancing corporate governance.

💡 Core Provisions for Enterprises:

  • Simplified Joint Stock Company: A flexible, innovative corporate form that requires no minimum capital and can be managed by one or more directors without standard board complications.
  • Removal of Single-Shareholder Limits: Individuals (natural or legal) are permitted to establish joint stock and LLC entities directly without requiring token partners.
  • Family Charters: Institutionalizing family business governance and regulating transition of generations to prevent conflicts and ensure business longevity.

Labor & Employment

Saudi Labor Law (1426H / 2005G & Amendments)

Regulates the contractual and operational relations between private sector employers and employees, defining specific rights and duties to ensure stable, conflict-free environments.

💡 Core Provisions for Business Establishments:

  • Written Arab Contracts: Article 51 requires all employment contracts to be written in Arabic to secure statutory validity and avoid translation interpretation loopholes.
  • End of Service Benefits (Article 84): Entitles employees to a benefit of half a month's actual pay for each of the first five years, and a full month's pay for each subsequent year.
  • Lawful Termination & Article 77: Protecting enterprises from unlawful termination damage claims by systematically deploying warning processes and evaluation records.

Litigation & Courts

Saudi Commercial Court Law (1441H / 2020G)

Focuses on accelerating resolution in commercial disputes, activating digital courts, and strictly acknowledging settlement agreements and private commercial arbitration.

💡 Dispute Resolution Speed for Companies:

  • Commercial Payment Orders: A fast-track legal route enabling creditors to directly execute on debt if the right is written and uncontested, avoiding lengthy court trials.
  • Mandatory Mediation: Requiring mandatory recourse to settlement or mediation for specific corporate disputes before presenting them to the commercial court.
  • Electronic Litigation: Enabling digital court sessions, remote pleading, and electronic submission of objection briefs to secure prompt enforcement.

Enforcement Court

Saudi Enforcement Law (1434H / 2012G & Amendments)

Ensures immediate execution of judgements and deeds through direct assets attachment, protecting corporate credit transactions in Saudi Arabia.

💡 Executive Deeds & Asset Protections:

  • Executive Deeds: Promissory notes, drafts, certified contracts, and unified rental contracts directly act as executive deeds for fast-track collections.
  • Coercive Actions (Article 46): Empowers enforcement judges to freeze bank accounts, attach properties, and issue travel bans within 5 days of notice.
  • Asset Tracing: Robust tracing mechanisms through central bank structures and all state asset registries to seize values from non-paying debtors.

Law of Evidence

Saudi Law of Evidence (1443H / 2021G)

Regulates tools for presenting evidence in commercial transactions, explicitly admitting electronic and digital records as definitive judicial proofs.

💡 Digital Evidence Validity & Strict Documentation:

  • Electronic Proofs: Emails, digital messages, and certified WhatsApp records are fully accepted as legal evidence.
  • Contractual Formality: Restricting the use of witness testimony for transactions exceeding 100,000 SAR, requiring written contracts.
  • Strict Oaths & Expert Testimony: Overhauling judicial oath requests and regulatory rules for expert testimonies.
