Drafting Partnership Agreements under Saudi New Companies Law

A comprehensive guide for business owners and founders to protect assets and avoid costly legal disputes.

Forming a commercial partnership is a powerful strategic step to scale your business. However, the lack of legal clarity in governing the relationship between partners is a leading cause of corporate dissolution in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. With the enactment of the **New Saudi Companies Law** by the Ministry of Commerce, it has become essential to draft compliant articles of association and shareholder agreements to secure everyone's interests.

The Importance of Drafting under the New Law

A partnership agreement is no longer just a boilerplate template used to extract a commercial register. The new Companies Law allows incredible flexibility in structuring agreements, such as joint-venture capital, partnerships by labor, and simplified joint-stock companies. Professional drafting defines administrative powers, preventing deadlocks and securing shareholder rights from day one.

Key Clauses Every Partnership Agreement Needs

  • Detailed Capital Contribution: Clear specifications of the monetary or in-kind assets contributed by each partner, as well as labor contributions.
  • Management & Signing Authority: Identifying who governs the company, signs contracts, and appoints directors, along with financial limits of authority.
  • Profit/Loss Distribution Policy: Clear mechanisms to calculate annual profits, distribute dividends, and fund deficit capital.
  • Share Transfer Restrictions (Lock-up): Specifying guidelines for selling shares to external parties, along with rights of first refusal for existing partners.
  • Non-Compete Clauses: Prohibiting partners from operating similar or competing entities during and after the partnership for a set period.

Dispute Resolution & Exit Strategies

A successful contract anticipates disagreements before they arise. Agreements must outline clear protocols for amicable settlements, mediation, or referring conflicts to **Commercial Arbitration** as a fast-track alternative to general courts, which can freeze company bank accounts for months.
