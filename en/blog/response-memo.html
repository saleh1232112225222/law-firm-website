What is a Statement of Defense and How to Write It Properly?

A complete guide to the statement of defense (memorandum of response) in Saudi law: definition, acceptance conditions, proper legal structure, and key mistakes to avoid.

Published: June 18, 2026

The statement of defense is one of the most important procedural tools through which the defendant responds to the allegations in the statement of claim. It is a foundational document in litigation upon which much judicial assessment is built, and it can directly influence the course and outcome of the case.

In this article, we highlight the importance of the statement of defense, explain how to prepare it properly, outline the appropriate legal structure for drafting it, and review the key formal and substantive aspects that must be observed to ensure its acceptance and maximize its legal impact.

Many cases are decided in their early stages due to a weak or inaccurate statement of defense. Therefore, engaging a specialized lawyer ensures a legally sound response that complies with regulations and effectively defends your position.

1. What is a Statement of Defense?

A statement of defense is the procedural instrument through which the defendant responds to the allegations in the statement of claim. It is the defendant's first step in actively entering the litigation process. It should be submitted at the first hearing or within the deadline set by the court, and typically includes responses to the facts, procedural defenses, and any counterclaims.

The statement of defense is the cornerstone of the defendant's position, and all subsequent responses and defenses stem from it. If it is deficient in addressing the facts, silent on a critical defense, or weak in its legal foundation, it will directly affect the trajectory and prospects of the case.

Difference Between a Statement of Defense and a Rejoinder

A rejoinder comes after the statement of defense and is a response from any party to the litigation — usually the plaintiff — to what was stated in the defendant's statement of defense. Its functions include rebuttal, clarification, or reinforcement of a previous position.

Difference Between a Statement of Defense and an Appeal Memorandum

An appeal memorandum represents a transition from the phase of establishing a right to challenging a judgment. It is submitted to the Court of Appeal and does not involve re-examining the case from scratch. Instead, it addresses the correctness of the judgment, procedural errors, and whether the first court correctly applied the law.

2. How to Ensure the Court Accepts Your Statement of Defense

2.1 Comply with the Procedural Deadline

Submit your statement of defense within the specified timeframe. It should generally be submitted at the first hearing. The court may, pursuant to Article 57/3 of the Implementing Regulations of the Law of Procedure before Sharia Courts, grant an additional period not exceeding ten days.

2.2 Focus on the Core of the Lawsuit

Ensure the memorandum is a direct response to the statement of claim, relates only to the subject of the dispute, and avoids digressions or points outside the scope of the dispute so as not to weaken your argument or distract the court.

2.3 Clearly Formulate Prayers and Defenses

State your prayers and defenses clearly, whether procedural or substantive, and support them with relevant legal texts and regulations to strengthen their evidentiary weight.

2.4 Language and Legal Drafting

The memorandum must be in Arabic as per Article 2 of the Law of Procedure before Sharia Courts. Avoid inappropriate expressions or an emotional tone, and use clear, logical legal language.

2.5 Legal Capacity

The memorandum must be submitted by the person with legal standing or their legal representative, with a valid and effective power of attorney. The absence of a valid power of attorney renders the memorandum legally unrecognizable.

2.6 Method of Submission

Submit the memorandum by filing it directly with the court or through the Najiz electronic platform.

Customary Requirements for Acceptance

Beyond formal requirements, certain customary practices play a crucial role in enhancing the acceptance and impact of your memorandum before the judge:

  • Brevity without omission — excessive repetition weakens impact and dilutes focus.
  • Logical sequence — organize by facts, then responses and defenses, then prayers.
  • Support claims with documents — citing contract numbers, dates, and regulatory texts enhances credibility.
  • Clarify the primary request from the first page — avoid emotional language and unnecessary repetition.

3. How to Write a Legally Correct Statement of Defense

The statement of defense is drafted in an organized legal style, typically beginning with the basic case details: case number, names and identities of the parties, subject of the case, and the name of the competent court. This is followed by a brief preamble referencing the allegations in the statement of claim, then a systematic presentation of the defendant's responses.

Proper Legal Structure of the Statement of Defense

Response to the facts of the case: The facts are addressed as stated in the claim, either by denial, partial admission, or providing the appropriate legal context and interpretation.

Presentation of procedural defenses: These are divided into formal and substantive defenses, preferably supported by relevant regulatory texts to strengthen the legal argument.

Tip: The more organized, clear, and regulation-based your memorandum is, the greater its power to influence the course of the case and persuade the court of your responses.

It is well-established in legal practice that the memorandum should be concise, precise, free of rhetorical expressions, well-structured, and written in dignified Arabic that reflects respect for the judge and the judiciary. It should not contain anything that could be understood as offensive or unprofessional, and must be based on genuine documents.

4. Key Mistakes That Lead to Rejection

  • Submitting the memorandum outside the procedural deadline or without the signature of the authorized person or their representative.
  • Using weak or non-legal language, or unclear and disorganized drafting.
  • Lack of regulatory foundation or reliance on inappropriate provisions without precise legal connection.
  • Violating accepted form and content standards, such as failing to follow proper structure.
  • Ignoring responses to the facts of the case or critical defenses, or providing only a general, non-detailed response.
  • Raising requests or defenses unrelated to the facts or legal texts.
  • Contradiction between formal and substantive defenses without logical arrangement.
  • Attaching documents without explanation or clear connection to the defenses.
  • Omitting essential data such as the court name, case number, or party details.
  • Copying from previous cases without adapting to the current case facts.

5. Filing a Statement of Defense via Najiz

You can file legal memoranda and respond to them through Najiz by following these steps:

  1. Go to the "Request to Submit Memorandum" page on the Najiz portal.
  2. Click "Submit New Request".
  3. Select the case.
  4. Select "Requests" then "Add New Request".
  5. Select "Request to Submit Memorandum".
  6. Enter the request details and click "Submit Request".
  7. You will receive an SMS notification with the request number.
