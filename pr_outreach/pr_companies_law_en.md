## English Press Release Content

The investment and regulatory landscape in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia is undergoing an unprecedented historical transformation in line with the ambitious goals of Saudi Vision 2030. Successive legislative reforms serve as the backbone of economic diversification, designed to attract local and international investments. Among the most prominent milestones of these reforms is the enactment of the **New Saudi Companies Law**, which represents a major leap in organizing, protecting, and scaling commercial entities of all sizes.

In this context, **Attorney Saleh Al-Mohamadi**, a leading corporate law specialist in Riyadh, highlights that the new law introduces a highly flexible philosophy. It aims primarily to streamline procedures and reduce operational costs for entrepreneurs and startups, while providing maximum legal protection to shareholders and partners to guarantee the sustainability of economic entities.

### Unprecedented Flexibility for Startups and Foreign Investors
Al-Mohamadi points out that the new law introduces integrated legal solutions that were completely unavailable in previous regulations, most notably the **"Simplified Joint Stock Company" (Simplified JSC)**. This new model grants partners and entrepreneurial startups total freedom in defining their governance structure, drafting shareholder agreements, and transferring shares with minimal capital requirements and no complex restrictions.

Al-Mohamadi adds: "For the first time, the law allows the issuance of multiple classes of shares (such as preferred shares, voting shares, and non-voting shares). This provides companies with massive flexibility to attract investors and leverage diverse funding options without founders losing administrative control."

### Strengthening Corporate Governance and Minority Rights
One of the most important pillars of the new law is the consolidation of **governance and transparency**. According to Attorney Saleh Al-Mohamadi, the law establishes strict frameworks for the fiduciary duties of board members and executive directors (Duty of Care & Duty of Loyalty). It defines their civil and criminal liabilities clearly in case of abuse of power or damage to the company's interests.

Furthermore, minority shareholder rights have been heavily reinforced. The law grants them direct legal channels to object to unfair decisions and file liability lawsuits directly on behalf of the company against negligent management. This serves as a bulletproof safeguard for both foreign and domestic investors.

### Avoiding Partner Disputes: The Ultimate Legal Shield
In conclusion, Attorney Saleh Al-Mohamadi emphasizes that legal safety does not begin when a dispute erupts; rather, it starts from drafting and documenting highly customized **Articles of Association** and **Founders' Agreements** from day one.

"More than half of the commercial disputes involving partner deadlocks and corporate dissolutions that we handle at our firm in Riyadh could have been avoided entirely," Al-Mohamadi states. "This is possible if founders seek specialized legal counsel to draft sensitive clauses such as share transfer restrictions, amicable dispute resolution, commercial arbitration, and non-compete agreements. Investing in proactive legal security is the only true guarantee for your business to survive and thrive in the robust Saudi market."
