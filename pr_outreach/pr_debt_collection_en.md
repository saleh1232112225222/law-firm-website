## English Press Release Content

Cash flow and financial liquidity represent the lifeblood that ensures the survival and growth of any commercial enterprise. With the rapid economic expansion taking place in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, some companies face a growing challenge: the accumulation of **outstanding commercial debts** and customer payment delays. This can severely affect their operational and financial obligations.

In this legal guide, **Attorney Saleh Al-Mohamadi**, a leading specialist in corporate debt recovery and judicial enforcement in Riyadh, outlines a clear roadmap and highly effective legal procedures that allow companies to recover their outstanding receivables as quickly as possible without harming their commercial reputation or strategic partnerships.

### 1. Proactive Recovery: Proper Drafting of Commercial Contracts
Attorney Saleh Al-Mohamadi asserts that prevention is always better than cure in the business world. Successful debt recovery begins with the precise drafting of **supply agreements, service contracts, and commercial agreements**.

"We always make sure in our firm in Riyadh to include clear payment terms linked to tangible milestones, along with requiring the client to sign official, stamped delivery receipts (Deliverables Receipts) for each stage," Al-Mohamadi explains. "The absence of such documentation makes proving debt in court a highly complex and time-consuming process."

### 2. Amicable Settlement and Official Legal Notices
Before rushing to courtrooms, Al-Mohamadi advises taking a serious **amicable settlement** step. This includes appointing a licensed lawyer to issue an **official legal notice (Demand Letter)**.

"Receiving an official demand letter signed and stamped by a licensed lawyer demonstrates total seriousness to the debtor," Al-Mohamadi points out. "Giving them a specific grace period to settle or restructure the debt resolves more than 60% of cases amicably. This saves the creditor time, effort, and litigation costs."

### 3. Payment Order (Order of Performance) — The Rapid Judicial Tool
In the event that amicable solutions fail, Attorney Saleh Al-Mohamadi highlights an exceptional and extremely powerful legal tool provided by the Saudi judicial system: the **"Payment Order"**.

"If the debt is written, fixed, and due (such as a promissory note, a bounced check, or contracts and invoices clearly signed and stamped by the debtor), a lawyer does not need to file a standard commercial lawsuit that requires multiple hearings and verbal pleadings," Al-Mohamadi explains. "Instead, we submit a direct petition to the Commercial Court judge to issue an enforceable payment order in a matter of days."

### 4. Direct Enforcement Under Article 46 of the Saudi Enforcement Law
Once the executive deed or payment order is obtained, the request is immediately referred to the **Enforcement Court in Riyadh** via the Ejaz/Najiz digital portal. This is where the power of the Saudi judicial system shines in protecting rights by activating Article 46, which implements strict measures to ensure compliance:

1. Immediate disclosure and freezing of the debtor's bank accounts and assets up to the debt value.
2. Suspension of the debtor's governmental electronic services and commercial transactions.
3. Travel ban and financial transaction restrictions to prevent asset hiding.

Al-Mohamadi concludes: "Your company's receivables are the fuel of its sustainability. Being lenient in recovering commercial debts weakens your financial position and reduces recovery chances over time. Coordinating early with a law firm experienced in Riyadh's enforcement procedures is the key to accelerating your liquidity recovery and focusing on growing your business."
